Enigma

This collection is mostly an artifact from my Olympiad years. It contains some tricky math and physics problems that I have encountered or devised. They are chosen not for their difficulty, but for the "aha" moments they offer. Have fun!

  1. Find the optimal shape of a uniform-density planet with fixed total mass that maximizes the gravitational force on an object placed at a given point on its surface.
  2. Find a method to generate unbiased random bits (\(0\)s and \(1\)s) using a biased coin with unknown probability \(p\).
  3. Imagine a homogeneous, liquid planet of average radius \(R \) and density \(\rho\), rotating about its axis with an angular velocity of \(\omega\). Find its eccentricity.
  4. Two sealed envelopes contain different amounts of money. You choose one, observe its amount, and must decide whether to switch. Is there a strategy that gives a probability greater than \(1/2\) of ending with the larger amount, without assuming any distribution for the amounts?
  5. If \(A\) wins \(B\) with a probability \(p_{ab}\) and B wins \(C\) with a probability \(p_{bc}\), find the probability that \(A\) wins \(C\).
  6. Find the surface area and the volume of an \(n\)-dimensional sphere.
  7. A uniform rope of length \(l\) hangs between two walls separated by \(L\), with the right end \(d\) lower than the left. Find its shape and the position of the lowest point.
  8. A ballistic missile is launched from the north pole, the target is at the latitude \(\phi\). Find the required launch angle \(\alpha\) to have the minimal launch velocity. 2
  9. Describe the properties of a material that makes up a liquid planet, so that however much mass is removed, the radius of the planet stays the same.
  10. A helicopter requires power \(P\) to hover. An exact geometrically similar copy is built at half the linear scale. What power does it require to hover?
  11. Imagine two identical objects with heat capacity \(C\) and temperatures \(T_1\) and \(T_2\). If we were to build an engine out of them, find the maximum work that this engine can do. 1
  12. A point particle of mass \(m\) and charge \(q\) is moving under a homogeneous magnetic field of induction \(B\) and electric field of intensity \(E\), directed perpendicular to each other. Ignoring gravity and relativity, find its trajectory.1
  13. Consider a particle undergoing a one-dimensional random walk, taking steps of length \(a\) to the right or left with equal probability. Find the probability distribution of the particle's position after \(n\) steps.
  14. Consider a particle undergoing an \(n\)-dimensional random walk, taking steps in each direction with equal probability. Find the probability that the particle will come back to the origin.
  15. A person is collecting a set of distinct coupons, and each time they acquire a coupon, it is equally likely to be any one of the set. Determine the expected number of trials needed to complete the set.
  16. Calculate the repulsion force the light rays from the Sun apply onto the Earth.
  17. Find the oscillation period of a spherical ball oscillating in water.1
  18. Two small particles with opposing charges \(q\) are separated by a distance \(L\) and left free. Find the collision time.3
  19. A light source of intensity \(I\) and frequency \(f_0\) falls normally onto a mirror with mass \(m\), area \(A\), and initial velocity of zero. Find the frequency of the reflected beam as a function of time.
  20. A linearly polarised light beam transits from a medium with refraction index of \(n_1\) to another of \(n_2\), normal to the surface. Find the portion of the power that is reflected and refracted from the surface.3
  21. Imagine that the clouds are made of tiny water droplets that hang onto the air and distributed homogeneously in the space. Find the acceleration of a rain droplet that is falling down among them. The rain droplet, initially of negligible size, has a density of \(\rho\), and the water droplets in the cloud have a mean density of \(\lambda\). Assume the rain droplet absorbs all encountered water droplets while maintaining a spherical shape.
  22. Consider a rigid container from which all the air has been pumped out. Now, the valve of the container is slightly opened, and the air from outside is slowly filling in. The air flow stops when mechanical equilibrium is reached. Find the final temperature of the air inside. The room temperature is T, and the container walls are isolated. 2
  23. How far away from the handle of a sword of length \(L\) should you hit an object, so that you feel the impact the least?
  24. Imagine (1) an object with mass \(m\) (2) light ray approaching a planet of mass \(M\) from infinity, with initial lateral distance to the center of the planet \(D\) and speed \(v\). Find the shift in the heading direction after it escapes the gravity of the planet.
  25. Derive the approximation \(n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n} \left( \frac{n}{e} \right)^n\) (James Stirling, 1730).

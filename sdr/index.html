Weather Satellite SDR

I recently came across software-defined radio (SDR): with a small USB receiver and some software, you can listen to all sorts of radio signals. What caught my attention was the possibility of receiving images directly from weather satellites. Korea’s GEO-KOMPSAT 2A (GK-2A) is visible from where I live, so I decided to give it a try.

The first setup is a dipole antenna on a tripod, with an aluminum dish as a reflector. Simple enough to put together, but apparently not enough to receive the satellite’s weak signal at around 1.7 GHz. So far, nothing.

Next comes a homemade Yagi-Uda antenna, with dimensions chosen for the satellite’s transmission frequency. The signal is better this time, which is encouraging, but it looks like I need a larger reflector.

An old umbrella covered in aluminum foil seems worth a try. It looks a little questionable, but it finally works—after several frustrating hours of adjusting the alignment. Even a small change in direction can make the signal disappear, so once I find the right position, I am reluctant to touch anything.

Luckily, the satellite is about 49° above the horizon here, which helps with reception. The images come through in grayscale; after some post-processing, this is the result. It is quite satisfying to see an image of Earth arrive through an antenna made from things lying around.

The satellite sends images about every ten minutes. I am waiting for the weather to cool down a little so I can leave the setup outside for longer and collect enough for a timelapse. For now, I am happy to have it receiving anything at all.
